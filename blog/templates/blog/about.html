Hashen thesis

Bitcoin changes the balance-sheet question for private owners.

When an owner holds Bitcoin directly, through ETFs, through treasury-company equities, or as collateral, the basic question is no longer just “what is the portfolio worth?” It is “where is the Bitcoin exposure, who can move it, what policy governs it, and why does the position exist?”

Bitcoin is a monetary asset, a collateral asset, and a settlement network. That makes it powerful, but it also makes it awkward for traditional reporting: spot coins can sit in self-custody, ETF exposure can sit at a bank, treasury equities can sit in a brokerage account, and pledged collateral can sit with a lender. No single statement explains the whole position.

Hashen starts with reporting and governance because owners, boards, and advisers need a shared record before more complex infrastructure is useful. Month-end exposure, custody architecture, access rights, recovery paths, cost basis, concentration, and reserve policy should be understandable without reconstructing the story from screenshots and messages.

The first product is a private console for consolidated Bitcoin exposure reporting and decision records. It is not meant to replace a custodian, private bank, lawyer, tax adviser, or administrator. It is the owner’s source of truth for what the Bitcoin exposure is, how it is governed, and which decisions explain it.

From there, credit follows naturally. If Bitcoin is long-term reserve capital, owners will want ways to borrow against it without losing control of the balance-sheet story. That requires clean collateral records, policy limits, counterparty context, and reporting that can survive adviser, board, and tax review.

Settlement follows the same logic. A Szabo-style opportunity is emerging around Bitcoin as a cross-border payment and final-settlement rail: not as a vague crypto payments story, but as infrastructure for owners and businesses that need to move value across jurisdictions with clearer settlement assurances than correspondent banking often provides.

Hashen is built by Felipe Sick in Switzerland. The work combines owner reporting, Bitcoin treasury research, custody-governance documentation, and product development toward a broader Bitcoin-native capital infrastructure stack.

The near-term goal is simple: give private owners a clean monthly record of Bitcoin exposure and governance. The long-term ambition is larger: credit and settlement products that are easier to trust because the reporting layer came first.

Reporting comes first

Consolidated month-end exposure, NAV history, asset allocation, cost context, and share-price performance for Bitcoin-linked treasury equities.

Governance explains the position

Custody model, access rights, approval flows, recovery logic, key-person risks, partner roles, and reserve-policy decisions in one owner record.

Infrastructure follows

Credit on Bitcoin and cross-border settlement rails become more credible when collateral, custody, policy, and reporting are already structured.

Operating model

A focused path from records to rails.

Now

Consolidated Bitcoin exposure reporting, month-end PDFs, treasury-equity tracking, and governance memos for owners and advisers.

Building

Credit workflows around Bitcoin collateral, with clear records for loan exposure, policy limits, counterparty context, and reporting.

Research

Settlement rails for cross-border Bitcoin payments where speed, finality, and treasury integration matter more than another payment app.
